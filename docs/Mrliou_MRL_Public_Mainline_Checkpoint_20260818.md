## 1. Recording principles

1. Evidence first: facts, inferences, hypotheses, and future plans must remain distinguishable.
2. Preserve origin: original creator / original definition must not be overwritten by later implementation or platform ownership.
3. Preserve collaboration: real contributions by AI systems, tools, engineers, reviewers, or other collaborators must be recorded within their actual contribution scope.
4. Rights are not history: ownership, licensing, deployment authority, hosting, and commercialization rights do not retroactively rewrite provenance.
5. No-delete history: corrections append new evidence and resolution records rather than silently deleting the prior state.
6. Causal lineage matters: every promoted result should be traceable through cause, transformation, result, evidence, and next-state references.

## 2. Current mainline model

The current working mainline is organized as four connected lineages:

- A. Origin / Concept Lineage
- B. Engineering / Runtime Lineage
- C. Product / Commercial Lineage
- D. Evidence / Provenance / License Lineage

These lineages are connected but must not be collapsed into one category.

## 4. Attribution model

Public records should distinguish at minimum:

- Original Creator
- Collaborators
- Contribution Scope
- Evidence References
- Version / Timestamp
- Rights / Authority
- External Source / License, when applicable

AI systems or platforms may be collaborators, tools, source providers, interfaces, or execution environments. Those roles do not automatically make them the original creator or canonical authority.

## 5. System boundary

MRL Mother/Core, product implementations, prototypes, historical artifacts, external dependencies, licenses, AI contributions, and commercial assets are separate classes.

Public GitHub records are evidence mirrors / publication surfaces. They do not by themselves replace the private canonical mother storage, source artifacts, hashes, or ROOT acceptance process.

Before publishing future records here:

1. remove secrets, tokens, private credentials, personal data, and non-public licensed material;
